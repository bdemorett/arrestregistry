# California county verification notes (checked 2026-11-15)

Method: official domains only (.gov, .ca.gov, .us, county/court/sheriff domains). Several sites refused automated fetching (robots or client errors); those items are marked. The research run hit its web-search limit after 12 full counties plus court-only checks for 5 more. The remaining 8 counties are entirely TODO (see Gaps). Search results in this run returned titles and URLs only (no snippets), so no "(from search snippet)" facts are used. Facts marked "(title only)" come from an official document title seen in a search result, not from the page body.

General caveat: California sheriffs were on the 2026 ballot. Re-verify every sheriff name after new terms begin in early January 2027.

Statewide: Criminal court records are kept by each county's superior court; "Each superior court is responsible for keeping its own records" and requesters should contact "the clerk's office of the superior court where the case is filed" (https://courts.ca.gov/policy-administration/public-records). There is no statewide public superior-court case search: the Judicial Council lists search tools only for the Supreme Court and Courts of Appeal (appellatecases.courtinfo.ca.gov, supreme.courts.ca.gov/case-information) (same source). Remote access limits: under Cal. Rules of Court rule 2.503(c)(5), courts "may not provide public remote access" to criminal case records, but calendars, registers of actions and indexes are exempt from the case-by-case limit. That is why most county portals show only an index, register of actions or calendar for criminal cases and no documents. A presiding judge may allow remote access in extraordinary cases (2.503(e)) (https://courts.ca.gov/cms/rules/index/two/rule2_503). Statewide criminal history (RAP sheet): access is "restricted by law to legitimate law enforcement purposes and authorized applicant agencies"; individuals may get only their own record via DOJ Record Review: Live Scan with form BCIA 8016RR ("Record Review"), $25 DOJ fee (fee waiver available), and out-of-state applicants use FD-258 cards and BCIA 8705 mailed to Record Review & Challenge Section, PO Box 160207, Sacramento, CA 95816-0207. Record Review Unit phone (916) 227-3849; challenges use BCIA 8706; "Requests from third parties are not authorized and will not be processed" (https://oag.ca.gov/fingerprints/record-review). Arraignment deadline: PC 825(a): the defendant must be taken before a magistrate "without unnecessary delay, and, in any event, within 48 hours after his or her arrest, excluding Sundays and holidays" (quoted in Judicial Council benchguide https://courts.ca.gov/sites/default/files/courts/default/2024-12/btb_23_3e_2.pdf; the leginfo page https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=825.&lawCode=PEN refused fetch, so it still needs a direct check). Record clearance: dismissal under PC 1203.4 is by petition on Judicial Council form CR-180 (order CR-181). Eligibility: no pending cases and not on probation or parole; if probation was completed, "the court must grant your petition to dismiss"; if no probation was granted, wait 1 year from conviction (https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/clean-your-record/misdemeanors). Automatic relief: DOJ reviews records monthly under PC 851.93 (arrests) and PC 1203.425 (convictions), with "no petition or motion" needed. Effective July 1, 2022 (back to Jan 1, 1973); expanded Oct 1, 2024 (SB 731/AB 567). It "is not a dismissal, sealing or expungement" and DOJ does not notify individuals (https://oag.ca.gov/fingerprints/automatic-record-relief-penal-code-sections-851.93-and-1203.425). State prison locator: CDCR CIRIS https://ciris.mt.cdcr.ca.gov/; CDCR ID Unit (916) 445-6713 (https://www.cdcr.ca.gov/family-resources/how-to-contact-an-incarcerated-person/). WARNING: search engines also index a test host, ciris.mt-test.cdcr.ca.gov. Do not link it. Mugshot law: Civil Code 1798.91.1(b) makes it unlawful for anyone publishing booking photos "to solicit, require, or accept the payment of a fee" to remove, correct or not publish them; public entities may charge a reasonable fee to correct a photo (c); private right of action for the greater of $1,000 per violation or actual damages plus attorney fees (e) (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.91.1.).
