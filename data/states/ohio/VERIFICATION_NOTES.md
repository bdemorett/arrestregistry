Statewide: Court of common pleas "has original jurisdiction of all crimes and offenses" except minor offenses that go to lower courts (R.C. 2931.03, https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2931.03). In practice felony cases are filed with the county Clerk of Courts of Common Pleas. Municipal courts have "jurisdiction to hear misdemeanor cases committed within its territory" and may hold felony preliminary hearings before indictment (R.C. 1901.20, https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-1901.20). County courts are covered in R.C. 1907, https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-1907. A county can have several municipal/county courts, and each has its own clerk and docket. Statewide case search: none found. The Supreme Court "Case Docket" (https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/Clerk/ecms/) covers Supreme Court cases only. The Supreme Court's Courts by County directory lists trial-court websites but has no search: https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/courts/judicial-system/ohio-trial-courts/ . ohio.gov Local Courts page (no search tool): https://ohio.gov/government/resources/ohio-courts . re:SearchOH (https://researchoh.tylerhost.net/CourtRecordsSearch/Home#!/home) is a Tyler Technologies vendor portal. It covers only participating courts (Lucas County added General Division cases Jan 6, 2026, per https://co.lucas.oh.us/3707/Online-Dockets), requires an account, and is not a state-run statewide search. Criminal history: BCI says "Computerized criminal history records maintained by the Bureau of Criminal Investigation (BCI) are not public record". To get your own record you need a full fingerprint set (WebCheck provider, or BCI London/Boardman/Bowling Green), a signed consent form, and $22 payable to "Treasurer of State of Ohio"; a WebCheck vendor may add a fee (https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Individuals-and-Families/Consumers/Requesting-Your-Own-Criminal-Records ; WebCheck https://ohioattorneygeneral.gov/Business/Services-for-Business/WebCheck ; locations https://www.ohioattorneygeneral.gov/webcheck). There is no public name search of BCI records. Initial appearance: Crim.R. 4(E)(2) says a person arrested without a warrant is brought "without unnecessary delay" before a court with jurisdiction. Crim.R. 5(A) covers the first appearance before a judge or magistrate. Neither rule sets a fixed hour limit (https://www.supremecourt.ohio.gov/docs/legalResources/rules/criminal/criminalprocedure.pdf). Sealing/expungement: R.C. 2953.32, current version effective Sept 30, 2025 (HB 96, 136th GA), building on SB 288 (134th GA). You apply to the sentencing court, or to common pleas if the conviction was out of state or federal. Sealing waits: F3 is 3 years after final discharge; F4/F5 and misdemeanors are 1 year; minor misdemeanors are 6 months. Expungement waits: misdemeanors 1 year (minor misdemeanor 6 months); felonies 10 years after the sealing date (about 11 years for F4/F5, 13 for F3). Convictions under 2919.25 (M3/M4) and 2919.27 can be sealed but not expunged (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-2953.32). Mugshot law: R.C. 2927.22 makes it a first-degree misdemeanor to negligently solicit or accept a fee to remove, correct, modify, or not publish criminal record information, including booking photographs. Each payment is a separate violation, and victims can sue for the greater of $10,000 or actual plus punitive damages. Enacted by HB 6 (132nd GA), effective Jan 18, 2018 (https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/chapter-2927 ; LSC final analysis https://www.legislature.ohio.gov/download?key=7757). NOTE: R.C. 2927.023 is NOT the mugshot law; it is "Unlawful transportation of tobacco products." ODRC Offender Search: https://appgateway.drc.ohio.gov/OffenderSearch ; Parole Violators at Large https://appgateway.drc.ohio.gov/OffenderSearch/Search/PvalListing ; ODRC https://drc.ohio.gov/ .
